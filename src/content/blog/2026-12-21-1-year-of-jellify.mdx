## If At First You Don't Succeed

I originally started
[the first version of Jellify](https://github.com/anultravioletaurora/Jellify---Swift/) back in
October 2021. It began as a native [SwiftUI](https://developer.apple.com/xcode/swiftui/) app for
Apple devices that synced the entire [Jellyfin](https://jellyfin.org) music library to the device,
and it even supported CarPlay. It _worked_, but only if you had an iPhone, and the database
synchronization really didn't scale.

Every sync was all or nothing. Tapping the sync button kicked off a cascade that pulled down every
artist, then every album, then every song, then every playlist and every track in each one, and only
saved once the whole thing finished. That's fine with a few hundred albums, but my library kept
growing, and the bigger it got, the longer I spent staring at a spinner before I could play the new
stuff.

Every sync was manual too. Add an album on the server, and the app had no idea it existed until you
went and told it to go look. Having to babysit my phone just so it knew what music I owned was
exactly the kind of chore I wanted a music app to take off my plate, not add to it. Your library
already lives on your server, so why should your phone have to keep a copy of it in sync?

I also quickly realized that I wanted to cover Android users as well, so I started over in March
2022 with [a React Native version](https://github.com/anultravioletaurora/Jellify-React-Native).
That attempt lasted about two weeks and 13 commits before I set it down. I never got close to a
working prototype, but it planted seeds that would sprout years later.

I took a brief hiatus from self-hosting in 2023, but it didn't last long, and I came back to it
stronger than before. At first I went back to [Plex](https://www.plex.tv) and
[Plexamp](https://www.plex.tv/plexamp/). When I eventually made the switch to Jellyfin, I couldn't
find a music app that worked the way I wanted it to, and so the journey to build one for myself
began once again.

The [current version](https://github.com/Jellify-Music/App) that exists today started on August
9, 2024. It's React Native again, but this time it stuck. Five months later, on January 10, 2025, I
tagged the very first release: version 0.0.1.

What made the third time different was that I finally figured out the formula: stop syncing the
database, and cache the API responses instead.

The first piece was [TanStack Query](https://tanstack.com/query). Instead of mirroring the whole
library up front, the app only asks Jellyfin for what you're actually looking at, whether that's an
artist, an album, or a playlist, and holds onto the response. The next time you open it, the cached
copy shows up instantly while a fresh one is fetched in the background. New albums just appear, with
no sync button in sight.

The second piece was [`react-native-mmkv`](https://github.com/mrousavy/react-native-mmkv), a blazing
fast key-value store. Persisting TanStack Query's cache to MMKV means those responses survive the
app closing, so launching Jellify drops you right back into your library instead of waiting on the
network.

Put the two together and I had a _eureka_ moment. All of the speed of keeping your library on
device, with none of the syncing. Your phone only keeps what you've actually seen, and Jellyfin
stays the source of truth.

## Alive and Amplified

I'm one developer who's partial to ~~JavaScript~~ _TypeScript_, up against a myriad of platforms,
and [React Native](https://reactnative.dev) lets me write one app for all of them.

TanStack Query handles the data fetching layer, but a music player has a lot more moving pieces than
that. Some of them didn't exist in the shape I needed, so I ended up building them myself. Here's
what's under the hood.

### The Frontend

The star of the show for the frontend is [Tamagui](https://tamagui.dev). I originally came for the
premade components, but I've stayed for the token system, and for how well Tamagui plays with the
rest of the React Native ecosystem, like
[Reanimated](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/). Everything from spacing to colors
pulls from the same set of tokens, so the whole app stays consistent without me having to hardcode
it.

#### Sliderama: A Reanimated Slider for React Native

For the player UI, I experimented with so many premade slider components before I decided to build
my own. None of them felt quite right under my thumb, and the ones that came close dragged in more
dependencies than I wanted.

So now there's
[`@jellify-music/react-native-reanimated-slider`](https://github.com/Jellify-Music/react-native-reanimated-slider),
built on the Reanimated and
[Gesture Handler](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-gesture-handler/) modules most React
Native apps already have. It handles both taps and drags, runs its animations fluidly, and can snap
to fixed steps. In Jellify, it's the seek bar on the player screen, and it's the stepped, springy
slider for the track lookahead setting. It's available via `npm` too, so any React Native app can
use it.

### The Backend

Jellify talks to your server through the
[Jellyfin TypeScript SDK](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-sdk-typescript), with TanStack Query
caching every response and MMKV keeping that cache on disk. App state, like your settings and your
play queue, lives in [Zustand](https://zustand.docs.pmnd.rs) stores that persist to MMKV as well, so
everything comes back right where you left it.

#### A New Kind of Player Module: Nitro Player

Jellify originally started off with
[`react-native-track-player`](https://github.com/doublesymmetry/react-native-track-player). It's
what I had used back in 2022, and it became the starting point for the current iteration in 2024. We
quickly outgrew it, however, when adding Android Auto and Google Cast support. We needed a player
module that kept everything in sync, regardless of the output target.

That module is
[`react-native-nitro-player`](https://github.com/riteshshukla04/react-native-nitro-player), written
by [Ritesh Shukla](https://github.com/riteshshukla04), who's also the second most active contributor
to Jellify. It's built on [Nitro Modules](https://nitro.margelo.com), and it handles the queue,
playback controls, Android Auto, and CarPlay. CarPlay support even landed in Jellify through
[Ritesh's own pull request](https://github.com/Jellify-Music/App/pull/1624), and it's far better
than the version I built in Swift all the way back in 2021.

## Special Thanks

### Jellyfinners

The [Jellyfin](https://jellyfin.org) developers gave me so much help, answering all of my questions
regardless of how naive they were. Huge thanks to
[Niels van Velzen](https://github.com/nielsvanvelzen) and
[Bill Thornton](https://github.com/thornbill), who undoubtedly helped me the most. They taught me
the ways of Jellyfin's audio API and how to transcode audio with it, and their help is what made
Jellify a reality.

The [Finamp](https://github.com/jmshrv/finamp) gang also lent a hand.
[James Harvey](https://github.com/jmshrv)'s
[blog post on the Jellyfin API](https://jmshrv.com/posts/jellyfin-api/) was exceptionally valuable
during development, and [Chaphasilor](https://github.com/Chaphasilor) taught me everything they know
about audio normalization and LUFS. Their math is the basis for Jellify's own audio normalization.

### Fellow Builders

Marc and the rest of the [Margelo community](https://discord.com/invite/6CSHz2qAvA) make the Nitro
modules Jellify leans on so heavily, and they've been just as generous with their support.

[URL polyfill](https://github.com/charpeni/react-native-url-polyfill) Jellify uses, and helped me
